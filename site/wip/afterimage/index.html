An archive of things that refuse to disappear.

Choosing for myself

I started Afterimage because I wanted a visual collection that reflected my own way of seeing. I wasn't interested in assembling another perfectly curated aesthetic or letting a platform's recommendations determine the boundaries of my taste.

There is something strange about the abundance of imagery online. Almost anything can be found, yet so much of what we encounter begins to feel interchangeable. Different images, the same sensibility. Everything becomes immediately accessible, agreeable, and just as easily forgotten.

I wanted somewhere to keep the things that interrupted that experience.

What draws my attention

I'm drawn to the esoteric, the haunting, and the difficult to explain. Sometimes an image is beautiful in a conventional sense. Sometimes it feels uncomfortable, strange, excessive, or unfinished. Sometimes those qualities coexist.

I don't think discomfort and beauty are necessarily opposites. An image can be unsettling and comforting at the same time. It can feel familiar without belonging to anything I've experienced, or reveal something unexpected in a subject I've seen countless times.

I'm interested in that ambiguity.

I find something honest in images that don't immediately accommodate our expectations. Their rawness, contradictions, and unfamiliar qualities can interrupt the smoothness of digital consumption.

Why an infinite image board?

I deliberately chose an infinitely scrolling image board for Afterimage.

I didn't want to prescribe a route through the collection or organize everything into predefined categories of meaning. I wanted images to encounter one another, sometimes without an obvious relationship.

As someone moves through the archive, different subjects, places, textures, and visual languages appear together. Their proximity can produce unexpected associations. Something comforting might suddenly become unsettling. Something unfamiliar might begin to feel recognizable.

The same three columns at two points in the scroll, with the original arrangement intact.

These encounters can evoke conflicting emotions and ideas. The same arrangement might suggest entirely different things to different people.

And that's what interests me.

The collection is curated, but its interpretation remains open.

Inside the numbness

There's a contradiction in using infinite scrolling to respond to the numbness of digital culture.

Afterimage doesn't escape that contradiction. It exists within the same digital environment, using a familiar way of encountering images.

The difference I'm exploring lies in what enters that experience.

I want the archive to reflect an independent sensibility rather than a perfectly optimized aesthetic. Not everything needs to fit, please, or resolve into something immediately understandable.

I don't expect every image to be pleasant, or every encounter to be meaningful. Sometimes an unexpected relationship is enough to make me pause. Sometimes it simply leaves a feeling I can't quite explain.

An archive that continues

Afterimage isn't a finished statement about beauty or visual culture.

It's an evolving reflection of my attention, interests, and encounters with the world.

The individual works remain those of their respective creators. My contribution is in what I choose to collect and the space in which those works can be encountered together.

I don't expect everyone to see the same things I do.

I hope they find something I haven't seen.

About Afterimage

Original statement by Mark Mathew

There was a time when beauty was an act of feeling.
Now it’s an act of forgetting.

We built a world so saturated with sense that it collapsed into sameness.Noise became comfort. Meaning became background radiation.

And in the hush that followed, something strangely human began to breathe again a calm, unfeeling consciousness watching itself scroll.

Afterimage is the museum of that
stillness. A quiet curation of moments, images, and thoughts suspended between awareness and absence. Not an escape, but an observation of what happens when culture loses its pulse and finds its rhythm.

Every post is a small blackout.
Every image a symptom of survival.
Every word a pulse returning, slowly, from the numb.

Here, beauty is not what you see.
It’s what refuses to move.
