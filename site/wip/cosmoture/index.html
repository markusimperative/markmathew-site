Cosmoture.

An experiment in making music a place we can explore.

PROTOTYPE
Guiding question
What if a music library were a place?
Year
2026
Form
Local-first Windows prototype
For
Listeners who keep their own music collections

Music as a time machine

I’m fascinated by how quickly a recording can return me to a place, a person, or a version of myself I’d almost forgotten. I hear the same notes, but I also hear something of the circumstances in which I first found them.

Recordings let us return to performances long after they happened. Albums and collections gave me a way to keep those encounters together. Streaming has opened up more music than I could ever have hoped to find. I’ve also noticed how easily it becomes the background to whatever else I’m doing.

I began wondering what it would feel like to spend time with a collection again, and to make a discovery without having asked for one.

Giving music a place

I began Cosmoture by asking what would happen if each recording had a place of its own. I call it a musical entity because it has a position, a little motion, and neighbours it can lead me to. I think of the collection as an observatory. I can move in close to one recording, then pull back to see what else gathers around it.

The distance has to tell me something. Two recordings might sit close because they share an artist or because they have a similar pace and energy. I want to be able to ask why they’re neighbours, and to disagree when a suggested mood makes little sense to me.

What the prototype can do

Engineering foundation · v0.4.0

I built the foundation around the music people already keep. In the Windows prototype, I can choose folders, read the recordings’ tags and artwork, and analyse the audio offline. The originals stay read-only. The library and layout are saved on the same computer.

The universe gives each available track a position to return to. Tempo and energy can influence how an entity moves around that position. I can pan, zoom or orbit the field, inspect a connection, and keep exploring while an album plays in track order.

I can also change a suggested mood. The prototype keeps my corrections when it analyses a recording again and uses them when calculating relationships.

Getting closer to listen

Proposed listening experience

I want to be able to travel through three-dimensional space and come up to an album. As I get closer, its artwork takes the centre while related recordings stay visible around it. I’m exploring how a halo around the artwork might carry playback controls and show how far through the recording I am.

As the recording ends, the view could widen and reveal a few nearby possibilities. I want that change in perspective to help me decide where to go next, while giving me time to stay with the album.

Cultural meditation

A personal collection can show how my tastes changed, who introduced me to a sound, and which places, languages or traditions I learned to listen to. I might hear an influence I’d missed, or realise that I’ve attached quite different memories to recordings that sound alike.

That’s what I mean by cultural meditation. I want to make room to revisit a period of my life, follow an influence, or sit with a familiar album long enough to hear it differently.

A universe still taking shape

Building the prototype let me try these ideas with actual recordings. I’m curious whether I’d return to the collection this way once the novelty of moving through it wears off.
